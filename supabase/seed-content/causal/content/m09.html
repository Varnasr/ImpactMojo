Module 9: Synthetic Control

Sometimes only one unit is treated: one state passes a reform, one city runs a programme. With a single treated unit and no obvious comparison, synthetic control builds one from a weighted blend of untreated units that tracks the treated unit before the intervention.

Building a Counterfactual from Donors

Choose a donor pool of untreated units. Find weights on those donors so the weighted average matches the treated unit’s pre-intervention outcome path and predictors. After the intervention, the gap between the treated unit and its synthetic version is the estimated effect. Abadie and Gardeazabal (2003) introduced it for the economic cost of conflict in the Basque Country; Abadie, Diamond and Hainmueller (2010) formalised it.

What Makes It Credible
Pre-period fit
The synthetic unit must track the treated unit closely for many pre-intervention periods. A poor pre-fit makes the post-period gap meaningless.
Donor pool discipline
Donors must be plausibly unaffected by the treatment and not subject to their own shocks at the same time.
Placebo inference
Re-run the method pretending each donor was treated. If the real treated unit’s gap is extreme relative to the placebo gaps, the effect is unlikely to be chance (Abadie 2021).

A natural Indian application is a single-state policy shock, such as Bihar’s 2016 alcohol prohibition, where a synthetic Bihar built from other states provides the counterfactual for outcomes like consumption, crime, or household spending.

Key Idea

Inference is by placebo, not the usual standard error. With one treated unit there is no sampling distribution in the ordinary sense. Credibility comes from the pre-period fit and from showing the treated gap stands out against placebo runs on the donors.

A good post-period gap with a poor pre-period fit is worthless. Always show the pre-intervention tracking plot. If the synthetic unit cannot reproduce the treated unit’s history, it cannot stand in for its counterfactual future.

Varna
Synthetic control sells itself in a single chart: the real line and the synthetic line on top of each other before the policy, then splitting after. When the pre-period fit is genuinely tight, it is the most persuasive quasi-experimental picture I know for a single-unit reform.

## R: synthetic control with placebo inference
library(tidysynth)
sc <- df %>% synthetic_control(outcome = y, unit = state, time = year,
        i_unit = "Bihar", i_time = 2016) %>%
        generate_predictor(...) %>% generate_weights() %>% generate_control()
plot_trends(sc); plot_placebos(sc)
Sources: Abadie & Gardeazabal (2003), AER 93(1); Abadie, Diamond & Hainmueller (2010), JASA 105(490); Abadie (2021), JEL 59(2).
