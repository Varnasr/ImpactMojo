Module 7: Regression Discontinuity

Many programmes assign benefits by a cutoff: a score, a population, a poverty line. Just above and just below the threshold, units are nearly identical except for the programme. That accident of administration is one of the most credible designs available.

Identification at the Threshold

A running variable determines treatment at a cutoff c. In a sharp design, everyone above c is treated and everyone below is not. Units just on either side are comparable, so the jump in the outcome at c is the causal effect, local to the threshold. Asher and Novosad (2020, AER) used India’s PMGSY rule, which prioritised rural roads for villages above a population threshold, as a regression discontinuity to estimate the effects of road construction.

Sharp, Fuzzy, and What You Estimate
Sharp RD
Treatment jumps from 0 to 1 at the cutoff. The outcome jump is the effect at c.
Fuzzy RD
The probability of treatment jumps but not all the way. Scale the outcome jump by the treatment jump (an IV at the threshold).
Local effect
RD identifies the effect for units near the cutoff only. A different threshold could give a different number.
Key Idea

The design is only credible if units cannot precisely sort around the cutoff. If villages could manipulate their recorded population to clear the threshold, the units just above and below differ in ambition and administrative reach, and the comparison breaks. McCrary (2008) gives the density test for exactly this.

Two validity checks before you trust an RD. First, the McCrary density test: no bunching of the running variable at the cutoff. Second, covariate continuity: predetermined characteristics should not jump at c. Estimate with local linear regression and a data-driven bandwidth; report bias-corrected confidence intervals (Calonico, Cattaneo & Titiunik 2014). A wide global polynomial is not a substitute.

Varna
RD is my favourite design to commission because the assumption is visible. You can plot it. If the picture shows a clean jump at the cutoff and nothing jumping that should not, a non-technical board understands the evidence in one slide. That clarity is worth a great deal in a funding conversation.
