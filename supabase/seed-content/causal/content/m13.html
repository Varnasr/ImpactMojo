Module 13: Capstone Project

The course's real test is not recalling estimators — it is choosing the right one for a live decision and defending the choice. In this capstone you design a complete evaluation for a programme of your choice, end to end, and write the brief that a funder, a government partner, and a sceptic would all accept. Everything below is the scaffold.

Step 1 — Choose the Design: A Decision Guide

Start from how treatment was (or could be) assigned, not from a favourite method.

If…Reach for…Key assumption to defend

Assignment follows a cutoff on a scoreRDD (Module 7)no manipulation; continuity at the cutoff

No counterfactual is possibleContribution analysis / process tracing (Module 12)theory of change; rivals ruled out

Step 2 — Write the Brief

Evaluation design brief — required sections

  1. 1Decision & question. What choice will this evidence inform, and what causal question follows from it?
  2. 2Estimand. ATE / ATT / LATE / effect-at-cutoff, and for whom — tied to the decision (Module 1).
  3. 3Identification strategy. The design from Step 1, with the one key assumption named.
  4. 4How you will defend it. The exact diagnostics — balance, pre-trends/event study, first-stage F, density & covariate-smoothness tests, pre-fit & placebos.
  5. 5Data & power. Outcomes and how measured; sample/clusters; a minimum-detectable-effect calculation.
  6. 6Analysis plan. Pre-specified primary outcome and model (a code skeleton), with multiple-comparison handling.
  7. 7Threats & robustness. Spillover, attrition, manipulation, weak instruments — and the robustness checks you commit to in advance.
  8. 8Ethics. Consent, equipoise, the ethics of the control condition, data protection.
  9. 9What it can and cannot conclude. Internal vs external validity; the decision the result will and will not settle (Module 11).
Worked example — a one-paragraph capstone sketch

Decision: should the state scale a school-meal fortification add-on? Estimand: ATT on haemoglobin for participating schools. Design: the add-on will be phased in across blocks over three terms — a staggered DiD with a Callaway–Sant'Anna estimator and an event study for pre-trends. Defence: parallel pre-term trends in haemoglobin; balance on block characteristics; robustness to the comparison set. Power: 60 blocks, ICC-adjusted MDE of 0.15 SD at 80%. Limits: identifies the effect for the phased-in blocks under parallel trends; external validity to other states needs a mechanism argument (anaemia prevalence, supply chain). A sceptic can see exactly where to push.

Your capstone
  1. Pick a real programme you know. Write the nine-section brief above for it (aim for two pages). Be explicit about the one assumption your whole design rests on.
    Marking guide
    A strong brief: (a) derives the estimand from a concrete decision; (b) chooses a design justified by the actual assignment mechanism, not convenience; (c) names the key assumption and gives a specific, runnable diagnostic for it; (d) includes a real power/MDE figure with clustering; (e) pre-specifies one primary outcome; (f) lists threats with pre-committed robustness checks; (g) states honestly what the result cannot conclude. Weak briefs pick a method first, leave the estimand vague, and assert assumptions without a test.
  2. Now suppose randomisation is refused by the partner. Re-design with the best feasible observational strategy and state precisely what you lose.
    Marking guide
    Look for: a defensible second-best (RDD if there's a cutoff; staggered DiD if there's a rollout; IV if there's a credible nudge; matching/DML only if selection is plausibly on observables), the new key assumption made explicit, the added diagnostics, and a clear statement of the cost — usually a narrower estimand (LATE, effect-at-cutoff) and a weaker, now-untestable identifying assumption.
