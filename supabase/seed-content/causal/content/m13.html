Module 13: Capstone Project

Pick a real causal claim, one you might actually need to defend to a funder or a board, and build the case for the design that can bear it. The capstone is not an exam. It is the deliverable this whole course exists to make you capable of producing.

The Brief

Choose a programme or policy and a specific causal claim about it. State the estimand precisely, name the design you would use to recover it, defend the identifying assumption, list the threats and how you would check each, and say honestly what the evidence could and could not establish. If no counterfactual is possible, build the theory-based case from Module 12 instead and say why.

Design and Defend a Credible Evaluation

1
Claim and estimand

State the causal question in one sentence and the estimand in potential-outcomes terms. Is it an ATT, an ATE, a LATE, a local effect at a cutoff? Justify which one the decision actually needs.

2
Design and identifying assumption

Choose the design from Modules 4 to 10, or the theory-based route from Module 12. Write the identifying assumption in one sentence and defend it. Draw the DAG.

3
Threats and checks

List the threats specific to your design and the diagnostic for each: balance, first-stage F, McCrary test, pre-trends and event study, placebo runs, stability across specifications.

4
Data and code plan

Specify the data you need, the unit and level of analysis, the sample size or power, and the estimator. Sketch the R or Stata you would run.

5
Honest limits and a one-page brief

State what the result could and could not establish, and how far it would travel. Distil the whole thing into a one-page brief a non-technical decision-maker could act on.

What good looks like

A strong capstone is honest about what the design cannot do. The weakest submissions over-claim. The strongest ones name the single assumption the whole estimate rests on, show how they would stress it, and tell the decision-maker plainly where the evidence stops. That judgement, not the cleverness of the method, is the mark of a practitioner who can be trusted with a research budget.
