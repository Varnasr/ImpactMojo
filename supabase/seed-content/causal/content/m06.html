Module 6: Instrumental Variables

When treatment is tangled with unobservables, an instrument finds a slice of variation in treatment that is clean, and uses only that slice. It is powerful and easy to misuse, in equal measure.

What an Instrument Must Do

An instrument Z shifts treatment but affects the outcome only through treatment. Rainfall shifts agricultural income; a school-construction programme shifts schooling; distance to a facility shifts take-up. Duflo (2001, AER) used the timing and intensity of Indonesia’s school-building programme as an instrument for schooling to estimate returns to education.

The Two Conditions and the Estimand
Relevance
Z genuinely moves D. Testable: the first-stage F-statistic should be well above 10 (Staiger & Stock 1997).
Exclusion
Z affects Y only through D, with no direct path and no back door. Not testable. It is an argument, and it is where most IV studies live or die.
LATE
With heterogeneous effects, IV recovers the local average treatment effect, the effect on compliers, those whose treatment Z actually moved (Imbens & Angrist 1994). It is not the ATE, and the complier group is usually not described.
Key Idea

The exclusion restriction cannot be tested, only argued. Rainfall as an instrument for income assumes weather affects the outcome through income alone, not through health, conflict, or migration. State every other channel and why it is closed. If you cannot, the instrument is not credible.

Weak instruments lie quietly. Bound, Jaeger and Baker (1995) showed a weak first stage produces estimates biased toward the confounded OLS result, with confidence intervals that understate the damage. Always report the first-stage F. A large second-stage t-statistic on a weak instrument is not reassurance.

Vandana Soni
When someone brings me an instrument, I ignore the second stage at first and interrogate two things: is the first stage strong, and can they close every alternative path from the instrument to the outcome. If the exclusion story takes more than two sentences and a lot of hedging, I do not believe it.
