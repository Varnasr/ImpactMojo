Module 10: Choosing & Combining the Practices

Three canons, one situation in front of you. The mark of skill is not mastery of any single method but knowing which the moment calls for — and when nonviolent method is not the answer.

A decision heuristic

If the situation is…Reach first for…
A charged conversation between rough equalsNVC — observation/feeling/need/request; empathy first
An adult needing to resist a young person's harmful behaviourNVR — presence, self-control, supporters, announcement
Harm has been done and a community must respondRestorative Justice — centre the harmed; circle/conference
The conflict is structural / about power and resourcesNone of the three alone — organise, advocate, change the conditions (see Gandhi flagship)

They also compose: an NVR supporters' meeting is run with NVC; a restorative circle depends on NVC-style empathic listening; NVC self-empathy is what keeps an NVR parent "striking while the iron is cold."

The selector is a starting point, not a silo: one incident can send you to NVR to stop harm, NVC to hold the conversations, and RJ to repair — in that order.

When each fits, and what it cannot do alone

A method chosen well still fails if it is asked to do the work of another. The value of holding all three is knowing each one's edge — the point past which reaching for it does harm.

PracticeFits when…Its limit — what it cannot do alone
NVCTwo parties can, in principle, meet as equals; the work is connection, understanding and a workable request.Empathy is not a container for ongoing harm. Where one party keeps hurting another, "hearing the need under the behaviour" without a firm limit reads as permission — and re-victimises the harmed. NVC connects; it does not, by itself, stop.
NVRSomeone must resist harmful behaviour from a position of care — a parent, warden, teacher — without escalating or retaliating.Stopping harm is not the same as healing it. NVR restores safety and the adult's presence, but it is deliberately one-directional; it leaves the relationship un-repaired and the harmed person's voice un-centred.

RJ repairs; it cannot be the thing that first makes people safe.

Three cross-cutting cautions

  • Power and safety first. Where there is ongoing abuse, a live threat, or a severe power imbalance, dialogue can endanger. Safety planning precedes any restorative or communicative process; sometimes the nonviolent act is to leave or to call for protection.
  • Trauma-informed practice. A traumatised nervous system cannot access OFNR or sit in a circle until it is regulated. Grounding, choice, and the option to withdraw are prerequisites, not niceties. (See the SEL flagship on regulation and vicarious trauma.)
  • The practitioner's own regulation. All three methods fail the instant the facilitator is dysregulated. Self-empathy and self-control are not preliminaries — they are the method.

Consent and voluntariness run through everything here. A coerced apology, a mandated circle, a scripted "request" that is really a demand — each turns a nonviolent form into a violent function. If participation is not genuinely free, it is not the practice.

Case — the hostel, and why the order matters

Priya is the warden of a residential-school hostel in Rajasthan. For weeks a group of Class 11 seniors have been "ragging" Aakash, a Class 8 boarder — petty at first (errands, mockery), then colder. One night it tips over: the seniors lock Aakash out on the terrace past midnight to "toughen him up", and he is found at dawn, shaken and mildly hypothermic. Aakash's parents are on the phone by breakfast; the seniors are defensive and half-proud; the junior dormitory is frightened; and a well-meaning colleague is already proposing "let's all sit in a circle and talk it out this afternoon." Priya has all three canons available. The skill is sequencing them.

First, NVR — because nothing else is safe until the harm stops. A circle held while the ragging is still live would put a terrified 13-year-old across from the boys who locked him out and call it dialogue; it would coerce a performance and re-injure him. So Priya does not open with a conversation about feelings. She establishes presence and a firm, non-escalating line: the terrace is locked at night, senior movement into the junior dorm ends, and she recruits supporters — two other staff and the seniors' own housemaster — so the limit is carried by a community of adults, not one warden's willpower. She announces plainly, without humiliating anyone back: "The ragging of juniors in this hostel stops now. I am not willing to look away, and I am not going to shame you for it either." NVR is deliberately one-directional here; its whole job is to re-establish safety, not to heal.

Then, NVC — for the charged conversations the incident has opened. Only once harm has stopped can the conversations be held without endangering anyone, and each is charged in its own way. With Aakash, Priya leads with empathy and choice — observation and feeling before any request, the option to withdraw at any point — so he is regulated, not re-interrogated. With the seniors, NVC lets her hear the needs under the bravado (belonging, status, their own history of being ragged) without excusing the act — the firm line from the NVR phase still holds. With the anxious parents and the frightened dorm, self-empathy keeps Priya regulated enough to stay present. NVC connects and prepares people; it is the bridge, not the repair.

Finally, Restorative Justice — because stopping and connecting have not yet repaired anything. Safety is restored and the parties are regulated, so a restorative conference can now do what neither NVR nor NVC could alone: centre Aakash's account of the harm, invite the seniors into genuine (uncoerced) accountability, agree concrete repair, and knit the hostel community back together. Run any earlier — before the line was firm, before people were regulated — and the same circle would have coerced remorse and deepened the wound. That is the why of the order: NVR makes it safe, NVC makes it speakable, RJ makes it whole — and each earlier step is the precondition for the next.
