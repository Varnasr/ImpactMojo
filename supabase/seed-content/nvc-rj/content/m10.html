Module 10: Choosing & Combining the Practices

Three canons, one situation in front of you. The mark of skill is not mastery of any single method but knowing which the moment calls for — and when nonviolent method is not the answer.

A decision heuristic

If the situation is…Reach first for…
A charged conversation between rough equalsNVC — observation/feeling/need/request; empathy first
An adult needing to resist a young person's harmful behaviourNVR — presence, self-control, supporters, announcement
Harm has been done and a community must respondRestorative Justice — centre the harmed; circle/conference
The conflict is structural / about power and resourcesNone of the three alone — organise, advocate, change the conditions (see Gandhi flagship)

They also compose: an NVR supporters' meeting is run with NVC; a restorative circle depends on NVC-style empathic listening; NVC self-empathy is what keeps an NVR parent "striking while the iron is cold."

Three cross-cutting cautions

  • Power and safety first. Where there is ongoing abuse, a live threat, or a severe power imbalance, dialogue can endanger. Safety planning precedes any restorative or communicative process; sometimes the nonviolent act is to leave or to call for protection.
  • Trauma-informed practice. A traumatised nervous system cannot access OFNR or sit in a circle until it is regulated. Grounding, choice, and the option to withdraw are prerequisites, not niceties. (See the SEL flagship on regulation and vicarious trauma.)
  • The practitioner's own regulation. All three methods fail the instant the facilitator is dysregulated. Self-empathy and self-control are not preliminaries — they are the method.

Consent and voluntariness run through everything here. A coerced apology, a mandated circle, a scripted "request" that is really a demand — each turns a nonviolent form into a violent function. If participation is not genuinely free, it is not the practice.
